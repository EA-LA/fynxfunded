# FYNX trading engine: readiness and how to connect it

The FYNX Trade terminal (`/trade/access`) and the trading engine behind it are built and tested. What's missing is the "fuel": a licensed price feed, a server to host the engine, and the owner's approval to switch trading on. This page lists exactly what to plug in.

## What is built

| Part | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine core | `src/engine/core.ts` | Validates orders (volume steps, price steps, stop/target on the right side, free margin, fresh quotes). Fills market orders at bid/ask and pending limit/stop orders on price. Values profit in dollars for every pair (converts JPY, GBP… through the quote book). Works out equity, margin, free margin and margin level. Closes at stop loss / take profit. Rolls the trading day at 22:00 UTC from the balance at the reset. Enforces the daily loss and static maximum loss limits by closing everything and locking the account. `targetReport` gives profit vs target, trading days, best trading day and the best-day (consistency) share, and a plain list of what is still missing; the profit target is reported once for review only when target, minimum days and the consistency rule (`consistencyPercent`, 40 on FYNX Funded) are all met with nothing open. `modifyOrder` changes the price, stop loss, take profit or trailing distance of a pending order with the same checks as a new order (price on the right side of the market for its type, levels on the right side of the order price). Trading hours per instrument: opening, changing and closing are refused with `market_closed` ("The EURUSD market is closed; it opens Sun 22:00 UTC") while the instrument's market is closed; cancelling a pending order is always allowed. While closed, `tick` fills nothing, hits no stop and checks no loss limit; on the first fresh price after the reopen a stop loss or stop order beyond a weekend gap fills at that price (not at the level), a limit fills at its price or better, and the loss limits are checked on that price. Swap/rollover: at each daily reset, positions held through it accrue the instrument's `swap` rate (USD per lot per night; none on Saturday/Sunday resets, three nights on the triple day, Wednesday by default); the swap stays on the position (in floating profit and equity, so it counts toward the loss limits) and is settled into the balance and the deal's `netProfit` on close, split pro rata on partial closes. `swap_applied` events. Trailing stop per position (`modifyPosition(..., { trailingPips })`, `Position.trailingStop`, MT5 convention): it starts once the closing price (bid for buys, ask for sells) is the distance in profit, putting the stop at break-even or better, then `tick` keeps the stop that many pips behind the best closing price, rounded away from the price to the tick, moved in steps of at least one pip and only towards profit (`trailedStop`, `stop_trailed` events). It can also be set in the order itself (`OrderRequest.trailingPips`): market orders open with it, pending orders carry it and the position gets it on the fill; `modifyOrder(..., { trailingPips })` changes or (with `null`) removes it while the order is still pending, with the same 5–5000 pip limits. It never moves on stale prices, while the market is closed or on a closed account; a trailed stop closes as `stop_loss`, and the entry risk keeps the original stop. Pending order expiry ("good till date", MT5 convention): `OrderRequest.expiresAt` (ms since epoch, whole milliseconds, at least 1 minute and at most 366 days ahead, pending orders only, refused with `bad_expiry` otherwise) is kept on the order; `modifyOrder(..., { expiresAt })` sets or changes it, `null` makes the order good till cancelled again. `tick` removes every order at or past its expiry before any fill on that step, by time alone (also while the market is closed or without a fresh price), emits `order_expired` and keeps the newest 20 in `AccountState.expiredOrders` (with `expiredAt`) so the terminal can show them. `tradingDayEnd(now, resetHourUtc)` gives the "end of today's trading day" expiry (the next 22:00 UTC reset). Price protection on market orders ("maximum deviation"): `OrderRequest.expectedPrice` (the ask for a buy, the bid for a sell, as the trader saw it) with `maxDeviationPips` (0–500, 0.1 steps; both or neither; market orders only, otherwise `bad_deviation`). If the fill would be more than that many pips worse than `expectedPrice`, the order is refused with `price_moved` ("The EURUSD price moved 1.3 pips against you (from 1.08000 to 1.08013), more than the 1.2 pips you allowed. Nothing was opened.") and nothing is recorded, so the same `clientId` can be sent again; the same or a better price always fills (`adverseMovePips`). This matters once a real feed and network delay sit between Confirm and the fill. Idempotent orders (`clientId`). All money uses `decimal.js`, rounded to cents. Pure functions: no clock, no I/O. |
| Trading hours | `src/engine/hours.ts` | `FOREX_HOURS` (Sunday 17:00 to Friday 17:00 New York, i.e. 22:00 UTC in US winter and 21:00 UTC in summer, US daylight-saving rule built in, no `Intl` needed), `ALWAYS_OPEN`, `isMarketOpen`, `marketState` (open, next open/close time, reason) and host-supplied `closures` (holidays, provider maintenance) with a plain reason. The terminal's open/closed badge, order panel note ("Opens Sun 23:00 your time · in 1d 10h") and the engine use this same schedule. |
| Types | `src/engine/types.ts` | Instruments, quotes, orders, positions, deals, rule sets, account state, events, `EngineError` codes. |
| Venue | `src/engine/venue.ts` | `LocalVenue` runs accounts: connects a `QuoteFeed`, loads/saves through an `AccountStore` with optimistic locking, serialises commands per account, ticks on every command, publishes engine events. `buildSpecs` turns the 45-pair catalogue into instrument specs from venue settings, with `hours` (default `FOREX_HOURS`; `null` only for tests), `hoursBySymbol` overrides and `swap` rates per symbol (validated; pairs left out charge no swap). `symbolMarketState(specs, symbol, now)` and `venue.markets()` (every pair's open/closed state, reason and next change at the venue clock) for `/v1/status`. `venue.schedule(days = 7)` gives each pair's open stretches and one-off closures for the next 7 days (at most 14) from `scheduleBetween(hours, from, to)` in `hours.ts`, for `/v1/status` `schedule`. `tradingConditions(spec)` / `venue.conditions()` publish each pair's `contractSize`, `minLots`, `lotStep`, `maxLots`, `leverage`, `commissionPerLotPerSide` and `swap` (`{ longPerLot, shortPerLot, tripleWeekday }` or null) for `/v1/status` `instruments`, straight from the venue settings. `rulesFromPurchase` turns an account's stored purchase rules (`profit_target_percent`, `daily_loss_percent`, `max_loss_percent`, `min_trading_days`, `consistency_percent`) into an engine `RuleSet` for `rulesFor`, refusing malformed values. Every snapshot carries a `report` (the target report). `venue.history(accountId, chunk)` returns one archived page (`HistoryPage`: deals oldest first, `chunks` = number of pages; read-only, never ticks or saves) from any `AccountStore` that implements the optional `archivedDeals` (refuses `no_chunk` outside 0 … chunks − 1, `no_archive` when the store can't read archives, `bad_state` for another account's page). `MemoryStore` (optionally archiving by deal count with `{ archive: { aboveDeals, keepRecentDeals, chunkSize, resetHourUtc } }`) and `StaticQuoteFeed` exist for tests only. |
| Host skeleton | `src/engine/host.ts` | `handleEngineRequest(request, options)`: the server side of the HTTP contract below, framework-free. Optional `limiter` (on by default in `createEngineServer`): too many requests from one user are refused with 429 `rate_limited` (see "Request limits" below). Checks the bearer token (`verifyToken`, e.g. Firebase Admin `verifyIdToken`), that the user owns the account (`canAccess`) and, for opening or changing orders, the account's `tradingEnabled` switch (closing and cancelling are always allowed). `GET /v1/status` returns your `status()` plus `markets` from the venue's trading hours, `instruments` (each pair's trading conditions from `venue.conditions()`) and `schedule` (each pair's trading hours for the next 7 days from `venue.schedule()`). Reads bodies strictly (unknown fields refused), maps `Idempotency-Key` to `clientId`, and returns engine errors as `{ error: { code, message } }` with the right HTTP status. Not imported by the website and not deployed by itself. |
| Timer step | `src/engine/runner.ts` | `EngineRunner({ venue, accounts, intervalMs, idleEveryMs, ready, onError })`: looks at every watched account every `intervalMs` (default 1 s) and right after new prices (`nudge()`, at most once per `minGapMs`, 200 ms), so pending orders fill and stops, take profits and loss limits close positions while nobody has the terminal open. Rounds never overlap (a nudge during a round runs one more round after it). Accounts with nothing open are checked only every `idleEveryMs` (60 s); `watch(id)` checks an account every round from now on and keeps it watched until it holds nothing, even if `accounts()` leaves it out. While `ready()` is false (price feed not live) rounds are skipped. A failing account is retried with backoff (1 s, 5 s, 30 s, 60 s) without stopping the others; a `conflict` is retried on the next round. `health()` (running, healthy = a round finished within 3 intervals, rounds, last round counts, failing accounts with their last error) and `executionMessage(priceFeedLive, health)` give `/v1/status` its `execution` and a plain `message`. |
| Server in one call | `src/engine/server.ts` | `createEngineServer({ pairs, settings, store, rulesFor, feed, accounts, verifyToken, canAccess, tradingEnabled, onEvents, runner })` wires the venue, price feed, timer step and HTTP contract together and returns `{ handle(request), status(), start(), stop() }`. `start()` connects the feed and starts the timer step (every new price nudges it); `handle` answers the HTTP contract through the host skeleton and puts an account that just placed or changed something on the every-round watch; GET `/v1/status` reports `priceFeed` from `feed.health().live`, `execution` only when prices are live and the timer step is healthy, and the matching sentence ("Waiting for prices…", "Order checks are delayed…", "Trading engine live…"). `stop()` closes the feed and waits for a running round. Not imported by the website and not deployed. |
| HTTP adapter | `src/engine/http-adapter.ts` | `createHttpHandler(engine, { allowedOrigins, basePath?, maxBodyBytes?, healthPath?, log? })`: a `(req, res)` handler for `node:http`, Express, Cloud Run or a Firebase `onRequest` function, so the engine runs on a real web server with no glue code. **Browser access (CORS):** answers the browser's OPTIONS preflight for the listed website origins only (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin` = that origin, methods `GET, POST, PATCH, DELETE`, headers `Authorization, Content-Type, Idempotency-Key, Accept`, 10-minute cache); any other website gets 403 `origin_not_allowed` and its request never runs; calls without an Origin (server to server) still need a valid sign-in. **Bodies:** JSON only (415 `unsupported_media_type`), at most 16 KB (413 `too_large`), malformed JSON 400 `bad_request`; uses `req.body` (Express) or `req.rawBody` (Firebase) when already read. `basePath` serves under a prefix (e.g. `/engine`). `GET /healthz` without sign-in returns only `{ ok, priceFeed, execution }` for uptime checks. Every answer is JSON with `Cache-Control: no-store` and `nosniff`; `log` gets method, path, status, time and error code (never the token). Tested through `HttpGateway` and on a real `node:http` socket. |
| Account directory | `src/engine/firestore-directory.ts` | Reads the engine's answers about each account (rules, owner, access, trading switch, accounts to watch, notice email) from the `accounts` / `challenges` / `orders` documents checkout already writes; read-only, cached; trading only with the host master switch and the account switch both on. See "Account directory". |
| Settings from the environment | `src/engine/config.ts` | `readEngineConfig(process.env, FOREX_PAIRS)` reads the venue settings and allowed websites from environment variables (table below) and refuses to start with one message listing **every** missing or wrong value; business numbers have no defaults. Holiday closures and swap rates are entered as JSON and checked exactly as the venue will use them. |
| Firestore store | `src/engine/firestore-store.ts` | `FirestoreAccountStore(getFirestore(), { collection: "engineAccounts" })`: the `AccountStore` for a Firebase host. One document per account; `save` runs in a transaction and refuses with `conflict` unless the stored `sequence` is the one the engine loaded (creation refuses if the document exists). Drops `undefined` fields (Firestore refuses them), refuses states over 900 KB (`state_too_large`, below Firestore's 1 MiB limit), refuses odd account ids and malformed stored documents (`bad_state`). **Long-lived accounts:** when a document would pass `archive.aboveBytes` (default 600 KB) the oldest deals move, in the same transaction, to numbered chunk documents `engineAccounts/{accountId}/history/000000…` (`archive.chunkSize`, default 250 deals each), keeping the newest `archive.keepRecentDeals` (default 300) in the account. The account keeps an `archived` summary (`archiveHistory` in core.ts: count, net profit, daily results, the last 1000 client order ids, first/last close) so the 40% best-day rule, retried orders and deal numbering work exactly as before; `archivedDeals(accountId, index)` reads a chunk back; the venue serves it as `GET /v1/accounts/{id}/history?chunk=n`. Set `archive.resetHourUtc` to the accounts' reset hour (default 22, FYNX Funded); `archive: false` turns it off. Needs only the Admin SDK's `collection().doc()` and `runTransaction`, so it imports no Firebase SDK and is not in the site bundle. |
| Price feed template | `src/engine/quote-feed.ts` | `StreamingQuoteFeed({ transport, parse, symbolMap, pipSize })`: the `QuoteFeed` for a licensed provider. The provider adapter only writes `transport.open(symbols, onMessage, onClose)` (WebSocket/SSE/SDK) and `parse(message)` → `{ symbol, bid, ask, time }`. The template maps provider symbols (e.g. `EUR/USD`) to FYNX symbols, refuses bad prices before the engine can fill on them (missing/negative numbers, bid above ask, spread wider than `maxSpreadPips` (50), timestamps in the future or older than the last quote), reconnects with backoff (1 s, 2 s, 5 s, 10 s, 30 s) and reports `health()` (`live` = connected with a valid quote in the last 15 s, accepted/refused counts by reason, reconnects, last error) for `/v1/status` `priceFeed`. |
| Price feed from settings | `src/engine/feed-config.ts` | `createFeedFromEnv(process.env, FOREX_PAIRS, { socket: url => new WebSocket(url) })`: for a provider that streams JSON over a WebSocket (most do), the feed is connected by filling in `FYNX_FEED_*` variables instead of writing code (table in step 1). It builds the address with the API key (URL-encoded; logs and reports show `•••`), sends the subscribe message after every (re)connection, reads symbol/bid/ask/time from the paths you give (lists inside a message, top-level lists, a `match` filter for price messages; heartbeats and acknowledgements are ignored), names all 45 pairs the provider's way from one format (`{base}/{quote}`, `C:{symbol}`, `{symbol_lower}`…) plus exceptions, converts seconds/ISO times, and reconnects when the provider closes, errors, or goes silent (`FYNX_FEED_IDLE_RECONNECT_MS`, default 60 s). Every price still goes through `StreamingQuoteFeed`'s checks. Settings are all checked before start, with every problem listed at once and the key never repeated. Tested against a fake socket and end to end through a venue (prices fill a market order, refused prices never do, reconnect after a drop). |
| Readiness check | `src/engine/preflight.ts`, `scripts/engine-check.mjs` (`npm run engine:check`) | Run on the engine host (or locally with the same variables) before going live. Lists in plain words what is set and what is missing: trading settings, price feed settings, Firebase access, the trading switch, the port; exit code 0 only when ready. `npm run engine:check -- --feed 20` also connects the price feed for 20 seconds and reports how many of the 45 pairs received prices, the last bid/ask and spread for each, pairs with no price, and why messages were refused (wrong pair names, wrong field paths, wrong time unit, wide spreads). It places no orders, writes nothing and never prints the key. |
| Host program | `src/engine/host-main.ts` (`startEngineHost`, `nodeListen`), `engine-host/server.mjs`, `scripts/engine-build.mjs` (`npm run engine:build`) | The finished engine server: connects Firestore, Firebase Auth, the price feed (`FYNX_FEED_*`), the account directory, the trade ledger and (with `FYNX_ENGINE_EMAIL_NOTICES=true`) email notices, then serves the HTTP contract on `PORT`. Refuses to start while a setting is missing (prints the readiness list), JSON log lines without keys/tokens/emails, a health line every 5 minutes (unwritten trade records tried again each time), trade catch-up on start (`FYNX_ENGINE_LEDGER_CATCHUP_DAYS`, default 7), stop on SIGTERM within 8 s. `npm run engine:build` writes `engine-host/build/` (one `server.mjs`, `package.json` with `npm start`, `README.txt`) ready to upload to Cloud Run. Deploys nothing. Tested on a real port in `src/test/engine-host-main.test.ts`. |
| Trade ledger | `src/engine/ledger.ts` | `EngineLedger({ writer: firestoreTradeWriter(getFirestore()), owner, pipSize })` writes every closed deal to the Firestore `trades` collection in the exact shape FYNX Funded's dashboard reads (`userId`, `accountId`, `challengeId`, symbol, Buy/Sell, lots, open/close times, pips, gross, commission, swap, `netProfit` with `pnlBasis: "net"`, `closeReason`, `source: "fynx-trade-engine"`, and for trades opened with a stop loss `stopLossAtEntry`, `riskAmount`, `riskPercent`, `rr` (planned, when a take profit was set at entry) and `rMultiple`; never written as 0 when unknown), so trades closed on /trade/access appear on /dashboard/trades (with "FYNX Trade" and "Stop loss" / "Take profit" / "Closed by you"…), in Analytics and in the account workspace. `owner(engineAccountId)` says whose trade it is (`userId`, `challengeId`, dashboard `accountId`). Record ids are fixed (`fynxtrade_{account}_{deal}`) and only ever created, never overwritten, so retries, restarts and `ledger.sync(accountId, state.history)` (re-send everything after an outage) never double a trade. Failed writes retry (1 s, 5 s, 30 s), then wait in `health().failed` (reason `write_failed`, `no_owner`, `unknown_symbol`) for `retryFailed()`; the ledger never throws into the engine. Pass it as `ledger` to `createEngineServer`: it listens to the venue, `status().ledger` shows its health and `stop()` waits until it has written everything. Note: the server function `evaluateAutomaticProgressionOnTrade` runs on each `trades` write; today it stops before changing anything ("fail closed"), so these records fill the dashboard and do not decide a phase. |
| Gateways | `src/engine/gateway.ts` | What the terminal talks to: `DisconnectedGateway` (default; nothing is sent), `HttpGateway` (the HTTP contract below) and `LocalGatewayAdapter` (in-process venue). `createGateway` picks one from `VITE_FYNX_TRADE_API_URL`. |

| Lowest equity of the day | `src/engine/core.ts` (`nextDayLow`, `DAY_LOW_STEP_PERCENT` = 0.1, `DAY_LOW_INTERVAL_MS` = 60 s; tracked in `tick`, written into `dayCloseRecord`), `src/engine/types.ts` (`DayLow`, `AccountState.dayLow`, `DayClose.lowestEquity/lowestEquityAt/roomAtLow`), `src/trade-access/daily-statement.ts` (`todayLow`, `closestIntraday`) | Every timer step (once a second for watched accounts) the engine compares the account's equity with the day's stored low, on fresh, tradable, fully priced equity only (nothing while a market is closed, a quote is older than `maxQuoteAgeMs` or a dollar conversion is missing). Write-light so Firestore writes stay low: a new low is stored at once when it is at least 0.1% of the day's starting balance below the stored one, when the account changes anyway in that step (fill, stop, close, reset, breach; no extra sequence step) or has no open trades; smaller dips at most once a minute. So the stored low is within 0.1% of the day's balance of the true low, and an account drifting down saves a handful of times, not every second (tested: 300 one-second checks → at most 10 writes). At the reset the day's record gets `lowestEquity` (the stored low or the closing equity, whichever is lower), when it was seen and `roomAtLow` (how close the day came to the higher loss level); older records without it show "—". The breach equity is kept as the day's low. `FirestoreAccountStore` refuses a malformed `dayLow`.    import { getAuth } from "firebase-admin/auth";
   import { getFirestore } from "firebase-admin/firestore";

   import { EngineNotifier, firestoreMailSender, storePrefs } from "./src/engine/notifier";
   import { FirestoreAccountDirectory } from "./src/engine/firestore-directory";

   initializeApp();
   const db = getFirestore();

## Account directory (`src/engine/firestore-directory.ts`)

`FirestoreAccountDirectory` answers everything the engine asks about an account from the documents checkout already writes, so the host needs no hand-written lookups:

| Engine question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Which account is it? | The `accounts/{id}` document id (`ft_…_p1`), the same id the terminal sends. Only `platform: "FYNX Trade"`, `accessVersion: 1` accounts exist for the engine; anything else is "Account not found". |
| `rulesFor` | The purchased rule snapshot on the account (`rules`, written by `provisionTradeAccess` from the agreement the trader accepted), via `rulesFromPurchase`, never today's public pages. Funded challenges (`challenges.status = "funded"`) have no profit target. A snapshot that does not match the account's starting balance, is missing, or uses a trailing maximum loss is refused (`bad_rules`) so the account goes to review instead of trading. |
| `canAccess` | The signed-in user is the account's `userId`. Any lookup failure answers no. |
| `tradingEnabled` | **All** of: the host's master switch `allowTrading: true` (e.g. `FYNX_ENGINE_ALLOW_TRADING=true`; off by default), the account document's own `tradingEnabled: true`, a paid order, the challenge `active` or `funded` (a `passed` phase waits for review), the account `active` and still the challenge's current account (`brokerAccountId`), and usable rules. |
| `accounts` (timer step) | Activated FYNX Trade accounts (`status` and `activationStatus` both `active`) whose challenge is running on them; re-read every 30 s; more than `maxAccounts` (5000) is reported as an error rather than silently watching some. Listed accounts the trader has not opened in the terminal yet are skipped quietly (`notOpened` in the round summary), not counted as failures. |
| Ledger `owner` | `{ userId, challengeId, accountId }` from the account, so closed trades appear on that trader's dashboard. |
| Notifier `recipient` | The trader's email from Firebase Auth (`lookupUser`), only when verified and the user is not disabled. |

It only reads (never writes), caches each account for 15 s (`cacheMs`) because the engine asks on every timer step, and `directory.invalidate(id)` makes a switch-off count at once. The query on `platform`, `status`, `activationStatus` uses equality filters only, so Firestore needs no extra index.

## HTTP contract (`HttpGateway`)

All requests use `Authorization: Bearer <Firebase ID token>`. Every response is JSON. A successful action returns a **Snapshot**: `{ state: AccountState, metrics: AccountMetrics, quotes: { [symbol]: Quote }, report?: TargetReport }`. `report` is optional; when it is present the terminal shows the progress card (LocalVenue always includes it).

**Missing prices (after a restart).** `metrics` never fails for a price that is not in yet. An open position whose own pair has no price, or whose profit cannot be turned into dollars yet (e.g. EUR/JPY without USD/JPY), is left out of the open P/L and listed in `metrics.unpriced` (sorted symbols); one whose margin cannot be valued is left out of the margin and listed in `metrics.marginUnpriced`. Both fields are only sent when not empty, so servers and terminals that do not know them keep working. While `unpriced` is present the timer step does **not** check the loss limits (a partial equity could close an account that is fine, or miss one that is not) and checks them on the first complete price; while either list is present `POST /orders` is refused with `no_conversion` ("Free margin cannot be worked out yet…, Nothing was sent."). Closing or editing a trade that can be valued still works, and the end-of-day record leaves equity blank. The terminal's account bar and the dashboard's live card show "Waiting…" / "Waiting for prices" for equity, open P/L, free margin, margin level and the room before the limits, with a sentence naming the pairs; the order review says new orders wait. (`metricsGaps` in `src/trade-access/live-account.ts`; tests in `src/test/engine-missing-prices.test.tsx`.)

Errors: HTTP 4xx/5xx with `{ "error": { "code": "no_margin", "message": "Not enough free margin…" } }`. The terminal shows the message as it is. Codes come from `EngineError` (`market_closed`, `price_moved` (409), `bad_deviation`, `bad_oco`, `bad_alert`, `too_many_alerts`, `no_alert` (404), `bad_notices`, `bad_trailing`, `bad_expiry`, `bad_settings`, `bad_rules`, `unknown_symbol`, `no_quote`, `stale_quote`, `bad_volume`, `bad_price`, `bad_stop`, `bad_target`, `no_margin`, `account_closed`, `no_position`, `no_order`, `conflict`), plus from the host skeleton `unauthorized`, `forbidden`, `trading_disabled`, `bad_request`, `not_found`, `method_not_allowed`, `server_error`, and from the Firestore store `bad_state`, `state_too_large`, and from the HTTP adapter `origin_not_allowed` (403), `too_large` (413), `unsupported_media_type` (415), and from the request limits `rate_limited` (429), and from the failed sign-in protection `too_many_failed_sign_ins` (429).

**Request limits (`src/engine/rate-limit.ts`).** Every signed-in user has three allowances, refilled continuously, checked right after the sign-in and before anything reaches the venue: `trade` (new orders, changes to orders and positions, price alerts, notice choices) 20 at once then 2 per second; `reduce` (closing a position, cancelling an order, deleting an alert) 100 at once then 20 per second, never tighter than `trade`, so "Close all" and getting out always work; `read` (every GET) 120 at once then 10 per second. Over the allowance the answer is HTTP 429 `{ "error": { "code": "rate_limited", "message": "Too many orders or changes in a short time. Nothing was sent; try again in 1 second.", "retryAfterSeconds": 1 } }` with a `Retry-After` header; nothing is recorded, so the same request (same `clientId`) can be sent again. A wrong token is refused (401) before the limits and uses no one's allowance. Change them with `FYNX_ENGINE_RATE_LIMITS` (e.g. `trade=10/1`; classes left out keep their default; `off` switches them off, not recommended), shown in `npm run engine:check`; refusals are counted in `engine.status().limits` and in the host's 5-minute health line (`requestsRefused`). Limits are counted per running engine process: with more than one instance each one counts separately, so the real allowance is that many times larger.

**Failed sign-in protection (`src/engine/sign-in-guard.ts`, in the HTTP adapter).** The request limits above start after a valid sign-in, so requests with made-up or expired tokens are not slowed by them. The adapter counts answers 401 `unauthorized` per visitor address; after `FYNX_ENGINE_SIGNIN_LIMIT` failures (default 30 within 300 s; generous because an office or phone network shares one address) that address is refused for the refusal time (default 900 s): HTTP 429 `{ "error": { "code": "too_many_failed_sign_ins", "message": "Too many failed sign-ins from this network. Nothing was sent; sign in again and retry in 15 minutes.", "retryAfterSeconds": 900 } }` with `Retry-After`, answered before the body is read or the token checked. `/healthz` and browser preflights are never refused; successful requests and other errors (403, 404, 400) do not count; failures while refused do not extend the refusal. IPv4 is counted per address, IPv6 per /64 network (`::ffff:1.2.3.4` is 1.2.3.4); memory is bounded (50 000 addresses). **It is off until you set `FYNX_ENGINE_CLIENT_ADDRESS`,** because where the visitor's address comes from depends on the host: on Cloud Run or behind any load balancer every connection comes from the proxy, so use `x-forwarded-for` (the address is read that many entries from the right, because entries further left are written by the visitor and can be faked; use `x-forwarded-for:2` with two proxies, e.g. a Google load balancer in front of Cloud Run); use `socket` only when browsers reach the engine directly. A request without enough forwarded entries is not counted. `npm run engine:check` shows the setting; the host logs `Too many failed sign-ins: address refused for a while.` (WARNING, with the address) and counts `signInRefused` / `addressesBlocked` in the 5-minute health line. Counted per running process, like the request limits.

### One cancels the other (OCO)

Two pending orders can be linked so only one of them can fill. Send `ocoWith: "<id of a pending order>"` when placing the second order (pending orders only), or `PATCH` an existing order with `ocoWith`. Both orders then carry `ocoWith` pointing at each other. Rules the engine enforces (`src/engine/core.ts`, tested in `src/test/engine-oco.test.tsx`):

- When either order fills on the timer step or a price, the other is cancelled in the same step: event `order_cancelled` with `reason: "oco"` and the cancelled order, kept newest first in `state.ocoCancelled` (20 kept). The fill email says which linked order was cancelled.
- Cancelling one by hand, or its expiry, keeps the other pending and removes its link (`order_modified`).
- An order links with one other order at a time; linking to a new partner unlinks the old one. Linking to itself, to an order that is not pending, or to an order linked with a third order is refused with `bad_oco` (HTTP 422).
- Linking is a change like any other: it needs `tradingEnabled` and an open market, like the rest of `PATCH`.
- The terminal: "One cancels the other" in the order ticket (pending orders) and in the pending-order editor, an "OCO" tag in Pending orders and a "Cancelled by a linked order" list.


## Run it (switch-on steps)

Everything is built; these are the owner's steps, in order. The repository never deploys the engine.

1. **Build the server folder:** `npm ci && npm run engine:build` → `engine-host/build/` with `server.mjs` (the whole engine), `package.json` (`npm start`, Node 22+, `firebase-admin` at the version the Firebase functions use) and `README.txt`. The folder is ignored by git; rebuild after pulling new code.
2. **Fill in the settings** on the host (Cloud Run → Edit & deploy new revision → Variables & Secrets): the `FYNX_ENGINE_*` table in step 4 above and the `FYNX_FEED_*` table in step 1. `FYNX_FEED_API_KEY` goes in Secrets. Optional: `FYNX_ENGINE_EMAIL_NOTICES=true` once an email sender reads the `mail` collection; `FYNX_ENGINE_LEDGER_CATCHUP_DAYS` (0–30, default 7, 0 = off) for how far back trades are re-sent to the dashboard on start. Set `FYNX_ENGINE_CLIENT_ADDRESS=x-forwarded-for` on Cloud Run to switch on the failed sign-in protection. Leave `FYNX_ENGINE_ALLOW_TRADING` unset for now.
3. **Check them** from your computer with the same values: `npm run engine:check -- --feed 20`. Everything must say `[ready]` and prices must arrive.
4. **Start it** on Cloud Run: `gcloud run deploy fynx-engine --source engine-host/build --region <region> --min-instances 1 --no-cpu-throttling --service-account <account with Firestore + Firebase Auth access>` (minimum 1 instance with CPU always on, because the timer step checks stops and pending orders with nobody online). On any other Node 22+ server: `cd engine-host/build && npm install && npm start`. A missing setting stops it at once with the list in the logs; nothing half-starts.
5. **Look at it:** `https://<service address>/healthz` must show `"priceFeed": true`. The logs show `FYNX trading engine started.` with the price address (key shown as •••), the allowed websites and `trading: off`.
6. **Point the website at it:** set `VITE_FYNX_TRADE_API_URL` to the service address in Vercel and add it to `connect-src` in `vercel.json` (step 6 above). The terminal then shows real prices, accounts, alerts and history; orders are still refused.
7. **Open trading when you decide:** set `FYNX_ENGINE_ALLOW_TRADING=true` on the service AND `tradingEnabled: true` on each account document that may trade (step 7 above).

Stopping: Cloud Run sends SIGTERM; the server stops taking requests, finishes the current round and writes closed trades to the dashboard. If that takes longer than 8 s it stops anyway and logs how many trade records were left unwritten.

Restarting: nothing is lost. On every start the host re-sends the trades each watched account closed in the last `FYNX_ENGINE_LEDGER_CATCHUP_DAYS` days (default 7; newest 200 per account, 5000 in total) to the dashboard's `trades` collection (`catchUpLedger` in `src/engine/ledger.ts`). Records have fixed ids and are create-only, so trades already on the dashboard are left exactly as they are and only the missing ones are written. The log line `Trade catch-up finished.` says how many accounts were read, how many trades were checked, how many were newly written and how many were already there. Records that could not be written (Firestore outage, purchase documents not found yet) are tried again with every health line (every 5 minutes) until they are written.
